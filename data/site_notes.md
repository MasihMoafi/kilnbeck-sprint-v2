# Site Notes — Kilnbeck Brewing Co

*Founder's notes from two calls and one site walk with Tom Kilner (MD). Transcribed as said — treat like any customer account of their own operation.*

---

## The site

Independent brewery, North Yorkshire. Roughly 50 staff. Around 25,000 hl brewed per year. Brewhouse, fermentation hall, cold store, packaging line, offices. Electricity on a single site supply (half-hourly metered). The kettle and boiler run on **gas — out of scope for this exercise**.

A canning line was installed and commissioned **around May 2025** — "best thing we ever did, packaging used to be the bottleneck."

## Operating pattern (as Tom describes it)

- Production runs **Monday to Friday, 7:00 am to 5:00 pm**.
- "Nothing runs evenings or weekends. Last person locks up by six."
- Shutdown over Christmas — "we close between Christmas Eve and New Year, always have."
- Bank holidays: closed.

## Equipment notes

- **Cold store**: holds finished stock and hops year-round. "That one has to stay cold, no arguments." It has its own check-meter that the refrigeration contractor fitted — exports available from their portal (Tom shared a recent export, it's in the data room).
- **Air compressor**: "Old but reliable. Runs the keg washer, the racking line, valves, everything. Never give it a thought."
- **Lighting**: mix of old fluorescents and some new LED in the packaging hall.
- No solar PV, no battery, no EV chargers on site.

## Things Tom mentioned in passing

- "The electrician did some work in the meter cabinet **spring last year** [2025] — DNO were involved, took a few weeks to sort."
- Brewing records moved to a **new software system in September 2025** — "the export looks a bit different now but it's all there."
- On tariff: "We're on a fixed business rate. I'd have to dig the bill out." *(He never did. Decide how you handle this.)*
- On the bills: "They feel high for what we do, but I couldn't tell you why. Nobody here has the time to stare at spreadsheets."

## What Tom wants

"Don't send me a report I need an engineer to read. Tell me what's wrong, what it's costing me, and what you'd do about it — and be straight with me about what you're sure of and what you're guessing."
